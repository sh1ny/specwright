# Genre guide

Use this to set the game type id in the GDD's Genre and References subsection, and to raise the design questions that matter for that type. Do not read the whole table to the user. Match the pitch to an id, say which words matched, confirm, then ask the one or two questions marked **(H)** and the others as they become relevant.

**(H)** marks the high-stakes question that follows it in the cell. If it is unanswered in the GDD and not listed as an Open Question, the review raises a high-severity finding.

Ids are kebab-case and sorted alphabetically. The last column says whether narrative design usually needs `gamedev-narrative`: yes, light (a little), or no. Pitfalls include the anti-pillars the genre tempts a designer toward. Every list is a starting point; the user's design wins over the table.

| id | Signal words | Questions that matter most | Typical pitfalls | Narrative |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `action-adventure` | explore, fight, find tools, open the next door | **(H)** What is the player's main verb, and which secondary verb earns the pace changes? What does each new tool let the player do that they could not? How is the world gated, and by what? Is combat the point or the tax? | Padding between tool unlocks; combat as filler; gates that read as walls | yes |
| `auto-battler` | arrange units, watch it play out, synergies, rounds | **(H)** Where is the player's decision, since the fight runs itself? Can the player read why they won or lost? How do synergies combine without one always winning? How big is a round and a run? | Hidden randomness deciding results; one dominant synergy; fights too long to watch | light |
| `bullet-hell` | dodge patterns, hitbox, score chase, bosses | **(H)** How does the player read a pattern at a glance? What is the hitbox rule and is it shown? What does a hit cost? Where does mastery show in score or risk? | Visual noise hiding hazards; patterns that cannot be learned; difficulty spikes | light |
| `colony-sim` | manage settlers, queues, needs, base, disasters | **(H)** What does the player control directly, and what do the agents decide themselves? Where does failure come from and can it be traced? How does the player see what is going wrong? What ends a long run? | Opaque cascades; micromanaging dozens of agents; simulation depth the player cannot see | yes |
| `cozy-life-sim` | relax, routines, gifts, seasons, town, no failure | **(H)** What is the pull to come back tomorrow, without punishing a missed day? What does the player build or tend, and what changes visibly? Is there a pressure at all, and what? | Chore loops disguised as rewards; streaks that punish absence; no end or arc | yes |
| `deckbuilder` | cards, draft, build a deck, relics, synergy | **(H)** What does the player give up to add a card? What stops the best deck being a pile of the best cards? Where does randomness fall: draw, reward or enemy? How does a run end? | One card or engine that always wins; reward screens that decide the run; too many keywords | light |
| `extraction` | raid, loot, get out alive, risk what you carry | **(H)** What does the player stand to lose on death, and is it a choice? What makes leaving early a real decision? How does the session end cleanly? Is the stake the same for a new and a veteran player? | Stakes only the best can afford; waiting as the safest play; progress wiped with no learning | light |
| `fighting` | one on one, combos, frame data, matchup, rounds | **(H)** What does each action commit the player to (startup, active, recovery)? How does the player learn what beat them? What stops one move dominating? How is a new player given a way in? | Unreadable setups; no counter to a strong option; execution-only barrier | light |
| `general` | anything that fits none of the above | What does the player do most of the time? What decision repeats? What makes the player act again? Which genre would an outsider name? | A pitch that is a list of features; no stated verb | varies |
| `immersive-sim` | systems that combine, many ways through, emergent, simulation | **(H)** Which general systems (light, sound, physics, factions) interact, and does each interaction have a defined result? Does every goal have at least two real approaches? How does the world react to a player who does something unexpected? | Special cases instead of systems; one solution made obviously best; worlds that react only on cue | yes |
| `metroidvania` | interconnected map, backtrack, new ability opens old area | **(H)** What does each ability unlock, and how does the map tell the player where to use it? Can any state leave the player stuck? How does travel stay worth doing late? | Softlocks; backtracking without a payoff; abilities that matter once | light |
| `narrative-exploration` | walk, look, read, discover, a story told through places | **(H)** What does the player do between story beats, and is it enough on its own? How does the player know there is something to find? What is the pacing of reveals? Is there any failure? | Walking as the only verb; reveals not tied to movement; story outrunning input | yes |
| `platformer` | jump, run, precision, levels, collectibles | **(H)** Which movement numbers define the space (speed, height, distance) and how do levels derive from them? How is each new obstacle taught? What does failure cost in time? | Levels that ignore movement limits; hidden hazards; punishing retry | light |
| `puzzle` | solve, logic, rules, aha, levels of increasing complexity | **(H)** Is each puzzle solved by deduction, or by guessing? How is a new rule introduced and then twisted? Can the player see the state clearly? What happens when the player is stuck? | Rules the player cannot infer; one trick repeated; hint-free dead ends | light |
| `puzzle-platformer` | physics puzzles, move objects, reach the exit, precision plus thought | **(H)** Where does thinking end and execution begin? Is any solution decided by timing the player did not expect to need? How do rules combine in later levels? | Execution check where only thought was meant; unclear solution space; softlocks | light |
| `racing` | lap, track, speed, drift, opponent | **(H)** What does the vehicle model reward (line, braking, risk)? What keeps a lead interesting and a trail alive? Are catch-up effects visible to the player? How are tracks built from vehicle numbers? | Hidden rubber-banding; tracks that ignore the model; one best line | no |
| `roguelike` | run, permadeath, randomised levels, build, new run each time | **(H)** What does a death teach, unlock or reveal? What changes between runs because of the player's decisions? How far does power carry between runs, and does it add options or only raw stats? Is it a roguelike or a roguelite (persistent unlocks)? Where does randomness sit: the situation or the result of skill? | Meta growth that makes early runs trivial; randomness that decides outcomes; sameness by run ten | light |
| `rpg` | character, stats, quests, party, choices, levels | **(H)** What does a build choice commit the player to? How do stats change how play feels? Where does the player's decision matter in the story? What is the power curve per area? | Numbers that grow without changing play; fetch quests; dominant builds | yes |
| `shooter` | aim, weapons, cover, enemies, twin-stick, arena | **(H)** What does each weapon give the player that another does not? How does the player read where damage comes from? What does movement do against shooting? What ends a fight? | One weapon that always wins; enemies as targets only; ammo as a hidden tax | light |
| `soulslike` | stamina, commit to attacks, bonfire, lose progress on death, bosses | **(H)** What does each action commit the player to, and is it readable? What is lost on death, and what is the way back? Does the player see why they died? How does a boss teach its pattern? | Difficulty as the only identity; unfair telegraphs; long runs back before every attempt | light |
| `stealth` | sneak, guards, detection, shadows, non-lethal | **(H)** How close to detection is the player, and can they always tell? Is being spotted a fail state or the start of a new situation? Can each encounter be passed without violence, and does the game reward that? | Guards that react unpredictably; reload-on-detect as the only recovery; combat as the better option | light |
| `strategy` | armies, economy, territory, turns or real time, tech tree | **(H)** What decisions matter in the first, middle and late game? Is information shared fairly? What stops the leader snowballing? How long is a match? | One opening that always wins; information overload; late-game tedium | light |
| `survival-crafting` | gather, craft, hunger, build a base, night, hostile world | **(H)** What pressure keeps the player moving, and does it ever relax? What is the first hour's loop? Which crafts change play and which just add counts? What ends or resets a run? | Grind as the only difficulty; recipe bloat; pressure that disappears once the base is built | light |
| `tower-defense` | waves, place defenders, paths, upgrades | **(H)** What does placement decide that upgrading does not? Can the player read the wave before it arrives? What is the cost of a wrong early choice? Does a single tower dominate? | Upgrading as the only decision; one dominant tower; waves that can only be passed one way | no |

## Hybrids

When the pitch matches two ids, set the main one first and list the other as secondary. Raise the tension early, because it shapes the loop. Frequent pairs:

- **Run reset versus persistent world.** A run ends and resets, but the world remembers. Which state resets, which persists, and does the player feel both as one game? Decide what a death costs in each layer.
- **Precision movement plus permadeath.** Precise execution with a heavy loss on failure. Does a lost run feel like the player's mistake or the game's? How quickly can the player be back in the action?
- **Story under repetition.** A story told in order, wrapped in a loop that repeats. How does the story advance on each pass, and does the scene a player sees for the tenth time still earn its place? Hand to `gamedev-narrative`.
- **Draft layer versus encounter layer.** Choosing a build (cards, upgrades, party) and then playing it out. Which layer holds the decisions, and does the other become a chore? Can the encounter layer fail the build without the player knowing why?

## Notes on run-based design

Apply these when the game is a run-based roguelike or a close hybrid. All figures in the user's design are hypotheses to tune.

- Keep run power and meta power distinct. Prefer meta unlocks that add options over flat stat growth, unless deliberate power growth is the chosen fantasy.
- Every death should teach, unlock or reveal something.
- Randomise the situation, never the result of skill.
- Replay value comes from decisions that change between runs, not from randomness alone.
- Define expected power bands by depth, so the player and the designer can tell when a run is going unusually well or badly.
- Separate random streams per system (map, loot, enemies) keep a seed stable when one system changes.
- Ask whether the user means a roguelike (nothing carries) or a roguelite (something carries) before anything else.
